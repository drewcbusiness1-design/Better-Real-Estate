# Getting your first users

## First: why not to send a few thousand cold emails

You asked about blasting a few thousand emails from your Gmail. Here is what actually happens if you do:

**Hour one.** Gmail caps sending at roughly 500 messages per day, and trips its spam filter well before that on a new sending pattern. Your account gets rate-limited, then suspended.

**Day one.** Because the sending reputation attached to `drewcbusiness1@gmail.com` is now poor, your *legitimate* mail stops arriving. Signup confirmations. Password resets. Replies to actual customers. The transactional email your product depends on is the first casualty.

**Week one.** If you moved to a real email service and blasted from there instead, your domain lands on Spamhaus or Barracuda. That follows the domain, not the account. Every future email from `betterrealestate.com` goes to spam, including from a new provider.

**The legal part.** CAN-SPAM penalties run to about $53,000 per individual email. Enforcement realistically targets volume senders, but 2,000 unsolicited commercial emails is volume. The exposure is not theoretical.

**And the conversion math doesn't even work.** Cold B2C email to a purchased or scraped list converts at roughly 0.1%. Two thousand emails, optimistically, gets you two signups — and costs you your sending domain permanently.

The alternative below is more work per user and gets dramatically better ones.

## What actually works for this product

Your advantage is that you are inside the market you're selling to. Use it.

### Week 1 — make the site not look empty

Nothing else matters until this is done. A marketplace with no inventory converts nobody.

- **Post 10–15 real listings yourself.** Deals you're working, deals you've passed on, deals from your existing network. Ask permission where it isn't yours.
- **Your furniture and appliances are already seeded** — that's real inventory from day one, and it gives people a reason to come back even when they aren't buying houses.
- **Clear the sample listings** before anyone outside your circle signs up.

### Week 2 — the 50 people who already know you

Do not email 2,000 strangers. Message 50 people individually.

Every wholesaler, agent, investor and contractor you've dealt with. Text or DM, one at a time, in your own words. Something like:

> Built something I think you'll find useful — a feed for off-market deals where you set what you're looking for and matching properties come to you. I've got [N] live on there now including a [type] in [market]. Free to browse, no card. Want the link?

Fifty personal messages beats two thousand cold emails every time, because these people already trust you and will actually open it.

### Week 3 — go where investors already gather

- **BiggerPockets forums** — participate genuinely for two weeks before mentioning your site. Answering questions well earns you the right to mention what you built. Leading with promotion gets you banned.
- **Facebook groups** — search "[your state] real estate investors", "[city] wholesalers". Most have 5–50k members and permit sharing deals. Post actual deals from your site, not ads for your site.
- **REIA meetings** — your local Real Estate Investors Association meets monthly. Bring a laptop. In-person conversions are dramatically higher than anything online.
- **Reddit** — r/realestateinvesting, r/wholesalinghouses. Read the self-promo rules first; they vary and are enforced.

### Week 4 onward — content that compounds

- **Short-form video.** You already have a TikTok. Walk through a deal: "here's a probate in Austin, asking 268k, ARV 385k, here's how I'd run the numbers." The deal *is* the content. Post the calculator math on screen. End with the site name, not a hard pitch.
- **A weekly "5 deals" email** to people who *opted in* on your site. This is the list you're allowed to email, and it's the one worth having.
- **The marketplace is an SEO asset.** "Used HVAC condenser Hightstown NJ" is a search nobody is competing for. Each item listing is a page that can rank.

## Building an email list you can actually use

The difference between spam and marketing is permission. Get it:

1. Add a "get weekly off-market deals" checkbox at signup (opt-in, unchecked by default).
2. Put an email capture on the public feed for people who aren't ready to sign up.
3. Collect emails at REIA meetings in person.
4. **Referrals** are already built — $10 each side, paid on first purchase. Point people at it.

Once you have 200+ opt-in subscribers, move to Mailchimp/Resend/ConvertKit, set up SPF/DKIM/DMARC on your domain, and warm the domain up gradually (50/day for a week, then 100, then 250). Sent this way, email works extremely well. Sent the other way, it ends your domain.

## Realistic numbers

Do not measure against a venture-funded portal.

- **Month 1:** 20–50 users, mostly people you know. Zero to a few hundred dollars.
- **Month 3:** 100–300 users if you're consistently posting deals and showing up in communities. First recurring subscription revenue.
- **Month 6:** the inflection point, if it comes, is when sellers start posting without you asking. That's when the marketplace is self-sustaining.

The single strongest signal to watch: **does anyone come back on day 2?** Growth is not the metric that matters yet. Retention is.
